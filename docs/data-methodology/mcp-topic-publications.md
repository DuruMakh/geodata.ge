# Topic datasets in `/mcp` and the bulk files

This note owns how the Explorer's chart pages are served through the read-only `/mcp` endpoint and as bulk CSV files. It records the rules a caller needs; the reviewed data of each page is described in that page's own methodology note under `docs/data-methodology/`.

## What is served

Every Explorer chart page that is not one of the core datasets' pages is one **topic dataset**: 91 topic datasets beside the 11 core datasets (102 in `catalogue.json`). The dataset id is the page path after `/explorer/`, with `/` written as `-` (`/explorer/education/schools` is `education-schools`). Place pages (a region, a municipality, a city) are entities of the dataset of the national page they belong to. Topic data is annual; a school year prints as `2025/26`, and a value that comes from a household or labour survey is a survey estimate, not a count. The tool is `query_indicators`; `compare` and `rank` work on topic series and places, and `get_sources` resolves the sources a dataset cites.

A dataset serves exactly what its page shows: the page's series, places and years, never a row the page hides or a discontinued line. The value is the number the page's own model carries, to 12 significant digits (a sum the model adds up in floating point, such as 155199297.00000012, serves as 155199297; every page prints fewer digits), and a change `compare` or `rank` calculates on it is rounded the same way. Per-person figures, shares of GDP and rates that the browser calculates are not served; where the page itself sums rows, the value is marked `derived`. A missing cell stays missing (`availability: "missing"`), and zero is a value. Municipal codes `05`, `42`, `43`, `46` and `64` are never entities.

Each dataset is also one file, `/downloads/data/topics/<datasetId>.csv`, UTF-8 with BOM and without the internal `source_sheet`, `source_cell`, `source_workbook` and `source_number_format` columns. A missing cell is a row with an empty value and no basis, status or sources. A query that would return too many values is refused with the link to that file.

## Caveat codes added for topics

`topic_survey_estimate` (note), `topic_derived` (note), `topic_partial_period` (severe, limits comparison), `topic_planned` (severe), `topic_preliminary` (note, limits comparison), `topic_projection` (severe, breaks comparison), `topic_series_break` (severe, breaks comparison: a reviewed break inside the requested years, with its reason) `topic_dated_period` (severe, limits comparison: a value at a date inside the year) and `topic_general_government_scope` (severe, on every Spending by type answer: general government, not the state budget). The codes and their wording are owned by `ai-grounding-and-caveats.md`. A partial year's or a dated point's `valueDefinitionId` ends in its months (`:months=5`) or its date (`:date=2026-09-30`), as its definition does; `compare` still treats it as the same series, limited by its caveat.

## Change between two periods

`compare` and `rank` are the only places a change is calculated.

- A unit in percent (a rate or a share in %) changes in percentage points, never as a percentage, as for the core rates.
- A temperature (unit `celsius`) is on an interval scale: zero is not an absence. Only the absolute change is given, never a percentage change.
- A change across a registered series break is `not_comparable` in `compare` and excluded with its reason in `rank` (the 2024 census re-base of population, for example).
- A ranking of municipalities includes Tbilisi. The topic pages list it as the region `region.tbilisi`, which has no member municipalities, and it ranks as the 64th municipality, as on the page and in the core municipal rankings (code `04`). `withinRegionId: region.tbilisi` ranks Tbilisi alone.
- A ranking by series ranks like with like, for one place, in the unit of the series named (thousand persons never rank beside a rate, tonnes beside litres, GEL beside a share of GDP). A named series with a parent ranks among that parent's parts; one with none, among the series under its heading that have no parent either; a named total with parts ranks its parts, as `parentSeriesId` does. A parent whose parts make up several breakdowns ranks one at a time, that of `seriesId` when given, else the first: a breakdown is a heading of its own (trade partners by country and by country group, foreign investment by country and by sector) or a reviewed breakdown (students by level, ownership, sex and field; absolute poverty by settlement, age and sex; museum and theatre staff by sex, contract and age; each unemployment measure across its groups, so the labour force never ranks beside the unemployed it holds). A total never ranks beside its own parts. The totals pages print beside their parts without nesting them (Total running costs, Total consumption, All contributions, All staff, Museums) are linked to their parts in `apps/web/lib/factQuery/topics/hierarchy.ts`, and each build checks every link against the figures: the parts add up to the total at every place and year (within 0.01%, the rounding of printed parts), or, for a rate, share or average of the whole (real growth, the long-term unemployed share, the average social package, the poverty rate, towns' and villages' income), the total lies between them. A total that the figures do not prove so is not linked and can still rank beside its parts (Births beside Deaths and Natural increase, which is their difference, not a breakdown). A series under no heading and with no parent (the interest rates) has no peers and is refused with that reason.

## Known limits of the served groups

- **Combined historical regions.** The two combined historical region groups in the labour and unemployment data are typed `region`, with no historical marker. They are not current regions and do not add up with the eleven regions.
- **Trade country groups.** The trade partner groups (EU, CIS and the like) share a parent with the individual countries but sit under their own heading, and they overlap the countries they contain. `rank` by that parent ranks the countries only; the groups rank among themselves (a `seriesId` under the groups' heading).
