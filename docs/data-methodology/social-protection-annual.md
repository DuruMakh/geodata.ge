# Annual social protection data collection and validation

## Purpose and status

This methodology governs the 22,087 values of `data/imports/social-protection-annual.csv`: 21,988 built from the Social Service Agency (SSA) research package at `docs/Raw Data/Social/ssa-statistics/` (collected 16–18 October 2026), served on the Social payments, Pensions, Subsistence allowance and Social package pages of the Health and social protection hub under the approved design `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-20-social-protection-design.md`, and 99 built from the Pension Fund of Georgia research package at `docs/Raw Data/Social/pension-fund/` (collected 21 October 2026), served on the Funded pension page under `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-funded-pension-design.md`. The research captures are immutable. Nothing is published through MCP or included in central machine-readable publications, and the SSA sources are excluded from the fact-query snapshot.

Annual data only, 2013–2025 (SSA) and 2019–2025 (the Pension Fund). 2026 is left out until its 12 months (SSA) or its statements (the Pension Fund) are published. Children and maternity pages, the IDP allowance page, a table of the small programmes, care institutions, disability healthcare, pensions by age and sex, the subsistence allowance by sex, household size or score band, monthly figures, pre-2013 data and budget comparisons are excluded.

## Sources and coverage

SSA is the only source of the people and money served on the four SSA pages; the Funded pension page's sources are in its own section below. SSA publishes its statistics at `ssa.moh.gov.ge/statistiklist.php` as one workbook a year per table, with one sheet or column per month. Twenty-four tables supply values (`SOCIAL_SOURCES` in `lib/data/social/types.ts`): pension tables 1, 2 and 4; subsistence allowance tables 1, 2 and 3 (age groups); targeted social assistance database table 1 (`tsa_db-01`); social package tables 1, 2 and 10; and the tables of the other programmes on Social payments (two for maternity, 01 and 03 for 2013, and one for each of the rest). The Social payments total cites SSA's statistics list (`ssa_statistics`). All 25 are registered in `data/sources/source-documents.csv` as `source.ssa_*`.

Geostat's social protection tables (`docs/Raw Data/Social/geostat-social-protection/`) are a cross-check only: the 46 recorded December comparisons with SSA's national counts all match, and nothing from them is served. Geostat's municipal population (Population pages), headline consumer price index (Inflation pages) and nominal GDP (Economy pages) are the divisors of the calculated rows; they are already served and are not copied into the social file.

SSA renames its files on every upload, so source links point to SSA's topic pages, not to files. The public archive `data/methodology/source-archives/social-protection.csv` lists 23 originals: captures of the statistics list and eight topic pages (16 October 2026), the link lists of four more topic pages (18 October 2026), the four Geostat divisor workbooks with the ids and bytes of the Demography, Inflation and GDP archives, and the six Pension Fund PDFs behind the Funded pension page (`pension_fund_ifrs_2020`, `_2022`, `_2023`, `_2024`, `_2025` and `pension_fund_report_2025`, retrieved 21 October 2026). The 1,027 SSA workbooks (206 MB) are never in the archive or the methodology bundle (the PR #170 rule); `lib/methodology/sourceInventory.ts` reads only `official/pages/` and leaves out the pre-2012 link list. Each served table maps to its topic page's archive row by its topic (`socialArchiveSourceId`), which is how place-page and topic-page Excel cite it.

## The yearly rule

People and families are SSA's December count, as published (`months` 1). Money is the sum of SSA's 12 monthly "amount transferred" totals, added up by Fiscal.ge (`value_status=calculated`). A series starts at its first December (counts) or its first full 12-month year (money and averages): the high-mountain pension supplement has pensioners from 2016 and money from 2017, the social package severity groups recipients from 2017 and money from 2018, war participants recipients from 2019 and money from 2020, employment-programme families from 2022 and their money from 2023 (SSA prints it only from the December 2022 table). Social payments keeps every year SSA published; partial years keep their published months: workplace injury 2013 (9), child benefit for the third and later child 2014 (6), electricity tariff subsidy 2015 (5), the high-mountain pension and social package supplements 2016 (4 each), high-mountain child benefit 2016 (10), high-mountain doctors' and nurses' supplement 2016 (3), large-family electricity subsidy 2019 (6) and COVID-19 compensation 2020 and 2021 (6 each). The table and Excel mark them. All programmes counts what was paid in each year.

## Figures calculated by Fiscal.ge

SSA's own figures are the December counts, the monthly amounts and the social package group columns. Fiscal.ge calculates, and marks calculated in Excel: each year's money; the average per month (year's money ÷ the sum of the 12 monthly counts; for the subsistence allowance, regular money ÷ regular family months; for each social package group, the money paid to the group ÷ the group's recipients summed over the 12 months; an average whose monthly counts cover fewer months than its money is withheld, since a blank count is not zero: the national moderate-disability average for 2022, whose July count SSA left blank, and Upper Abkhazia's subsistence average for 2014, whose October count is blank and whose money is 0 anyway); All programmes (sum of the programme rows); people with disabilities in 2025 (sum of SSA's four disability "/ სულ" totals); a municipality SSA prints as two rows (their sum); and, on the page from served Geostat facts, the share of residents, the average at 2025 prices and Social payments as % of GDP.

- **Share of residents** = December count ÷ `demography.population_total` on 1 January of year+1 × 100. Population starts on 1 January 2015, so shares start in 2014; a place with no population has no share; excluded areas have none. Geostat recalculated 1 January 2025 from the 2024 census, so the 2024 share is marked with the census break and is not comparable with earlier years. SSA's own printed share in `tsa_db-01` is not used.
- **At 2025 prices** = average × (mean of 2025's 12 monthly headline `index_2010` ÷ the same mean for the year), the national index for every place.
- **% of GDP** = year's money ÷ served nominal GDP × 100.

People are never summed across programmes; money is, in All programmes.

## Subsistence allowance and the employment programme

From 2022, families with a member in the state employment programme keep their database registration and payment for up to four years. SSA counts them in a column of their own from December 2022, so from 2022 receiving families and people in them leave them out. This is recorded as `subsistence_employment_2022` in `data/imports/social-protection-series-breaks.csv` (demography's break format) on the families and persons rows, their shares and the age groups. `social.subsistence.employment_families` starts in 2022; `social.subsistence.employment_amount` (part of `social.subsistence.amount`) in 2023. "Registered in the database" counts every family and person scored in `tsa_db-01`, receiving or not. No people count is served for employment-programme families.

## Subsistence allowance by age group

SSA's subsistence allowance table 3 (`tsa_allowance-03`, "recipients by age group") prints, for every area row, the people in receiving families in eight age bands and their total. The bands are the same on every monthly sheet from 2012 to 2026: `0_6`, `6_18`, `18_30`, `30_40`, `40_60`, `60_65`, `65_70` and `70 და მეტი` (70 and over). They are served with SSA's band edges, as `social.subsistence.persons_age_<band>` (`0_6` … `65_70`, `70_plus`), with no regrouping; SSA's band edges share their end ages and SSA does not say which band an age on an edge falls in, so the labels repeat SSA's edges (0–6, 6–18 …). From December 2022 SSA prints the employment-programme people in eight bands of their own; they are not read, as `social.subsistence.persons` leaves them out, and the bands carry the `subsistence_employment_2022` break with the persons. A band heading the research package does not know, or a missing band, stops preparation. Each band is SSA's December count (`months` 1) for Georgia, every region, municipality and the two units, 2013–2025, on the same rows and area mapping as the persons; a municipality SSA prints as two rows is their sum (`calculated`).

The eight bands add up to `social.subsistence.persons` (table 1) in every place and year served. The research package's `validate_served` and the site's validation both fail on any difference, and the bands also have to add up from municipalities to their region and from regions to Georgia like every place series. In December no place differs. Four months that are not served do, and are recorded in `source-findings.csv` (`printed_total` holds table 1's persons, `sum_of_areas` the bands): in November 2023 the bands of Isani-Samgori, Tbilisi and Georgia are 1 more than the persons, and in October 2014 table 1 leaves Upper Abkhazia's persons blank where table 3 prints 0. The bands are listed under their own heading, Age groups, on the Subsistence allowance pages, never selected by default, in persons; no share of residents is calculated for them, since a band ÷ all residents is not a rate of that age group.

## Social package groups

SSA prints one column per eligibility group. `disability`, `survivors`, `war_participants`, `political_repression` and `other` partition SSA's total every year; the four severity groups (from October 2017) are parts of `disability`. In 2025 SSA redesigned the table: disability is split by severity and state-compensation recipients become a cross-cutting column, so `disability` 2025 is the sum of four printed totals and `other` changes from compensation recipients, utility-subsidy recipients and "other" to defence veterans and the social package by acquired right. This is recorded as `social_package_groups_2025`. In January–May 2019 SSA's money table heads column J "war participants" while the people table prints utility-subsidy recipients; the people table's heading is used, so that money is in `other`. War participants' 2019 money covers June–December only and is not served, so the 2019 money partition is checked month by month in the research package. Each group's average per month divides its money by its own monthly recipient counts summed over the year (the research package's `group.<group>.count_months_sum`, read with the same column mapping as its December count); those sums partition the total's sum too. A group's average starts with its money (severity groups 2018, war participants 2020). SSA's July 2022 national row leaves the moderate-disability count blank (socpackage-01 July E85, a `source-findings.csv` row), so that group's 2022 counts cover 11 months while its money covers 12, and no 2022 average is served for it (`SOCIAL_AVERAGE_GAPS`); blank is not zero.

## Places

SSA's rows use old district names and five combined Tbilisi districts. They map to the site's municipality codes and region ids through the package's reviewed `area-mapping.csv`; an unknown label stops preparation. Region values are SSA's printed region subtotal rows and Georgia's SSA's printed national total, never sums by Fiscal.ge. Tbilisi is `region.tbilisi` only. Akhalgori and Upper Abkhazia are kept as `unit.akhalgori` and `unit.upper_abkhazia`, part of SSA's Georgia total, never drawn and with no page or share. Municipal codes 05, 42, 43, 46 and 64 stay excluded.

## SSA's own sums

Printed totals that differ from their parts are served as printed, and validation allows exactly the recorded differences (`SOCIAL_KNOWN_DIFFERENCES` in `lib/data/social/validation.ts`): the May 2022 social package money total (socpackage-02 L85) is 56 GEL less than its group columns; the May 2021 social package people total (socpackage-01 M91) is 1 more than its group columns, so the groups' 2021 monthly-count sums add up to 1 less than the total's (a `source-findings.csv` row); IDP-status pension money in Shida Kartli 2015 (municipalities 404,686 GEL above the region); subsistence allowance money in Kvemo Kartli 2013 (126 GEL below). The last two recur in SSA's Georgia total (`source-findings.csv`).

## Funded pension

The Funded pension page (`/explorer/health/funded-pension`) serves the Pension Fund of Georgia's (საქართველოს საპენსიო ფონდი, the Pension Agency until 1 May 2025) figures for the funded pension scheme, 2019–2025, in the same canonical file and `SocialFact` mirror. There is no new table or migration. Nothing from it is in the fact-query snapshot, MCP or the central publications.

**Research package.** `docs/Raw Data/Social/pension-fund/` holds the thirteen original PDFs from pensions.ge byte for byte (six served, seven kept only for cross-checks), `source-manifest.json` (source id, URL, bytes, SHA-256, retrieval date and `used_for` for each), `extract.py` (poppler's `pdftotext`; runs once, offline, never in CI), `test_extract.py`, `pension-fund-annual.csv` (the 99 values with document, page and printed line) and `validation-report.json`. `npm run data:prepare-social` reads the CSV, verifies every PDF it cites against the manifest (`readVerifiedPackageFile`) and adds the values to the canonical file in the same columns; `--check` reproduces the file byte for byte.

**Sources.**

- Audited IFRS financial statements of the pension scheme, in thousand GEL (stored × 1,000): money for 2019–2025. Source ids `pension_fund_ifrs_2020`, `_2022`, `_2023`, `_2024` and `_2025`.
- The Fund's 2025 annual report (`Annual_Rep_2025_geo.pdf`, `pension_fund_report_2025`): members, people who left, people paid, employers and the annual return for 2019–2025.
- Cross-check only, not served: the 2019 statement and the annual reports of 2019–2024. The 2021 statement is a scanned image and is not kept.
- Geostat's nominal GDP, already served, for the % of GDP view.

**The served-document rule.** Each statement prints its year and the year before. A year is served from the latest statement that prints it as text: 2019 and 2020 from `2020-IFRS.pdf`, 2021 from `2022-IFRS-GE.pdf` (its comparative column), 2022 from `2023-IFRS-GE.pdf`, 2023 from `2024-IFRS-GE.pdf`, and 2024 and 2025 from `2025-IFRS.pdf` (in English). Counts and the return are served from the 2025 annual report, which carries the Fund's latest revision of older counts. A dash or an absent line gives no row, never a zero: dividends and securities have no rows before 2022.

**Measures and ids.** `social.funded_pension.{measure}`; money ends in `.amount`, so the existing million-GEL and % of GDP helpers apply: `savings.amount` (liabilities to members, 31 December), `net_assets.amount` (net assets available for benefits, 31 December; includes money received but not yet assigned to a person), `contributions.amount` and its three parts `contributions_private.amount`, `contributions_public.amount` and `contributions_state.amount`, `interest.amount`, `securities_gains.amount` (one net figure, negative for a loss), `dividends.amount`, `payouts.amount` (pensions and assets paid, including to members who left, stored positive); and `members`, `left`, `paid_persons`, `employers` and `return`. Units are GEL, `persons`, `employers` and `percent` (the new `SocialFact.unit` values are free text in the mirror, so no migration). Stocks and cumulative counts (savings, net assets, members, employers) have `months` 1; flows and the return have `months` 12. Every value is `official`; only the % of GDP view (value ÷ Geostat's nominal GDP of the same year × 100) is calculated, and a 31 December stock is divided by the same year's GDP. The workbook cites the statement or report of each value's `source_id`.

**Contribution split.** The statements print three parts (private-sector employers and staff, public-sector employers and staff, the state budget's 2%/1%), which add up to the printed total every year. The annual reports split contributions into private and public sector only, with the state's share inside each; the page uses the statements and the two splits are not compared.

**Annual return.** The 2025 report prints the yearly returns only as chart labels next to inflation. Each label is assigned to its year by horizontal position (`pdftotext -bbox`); the left bar of each pair is the return and the right bar is inflation. The returns for 2019–2025 are 6.1, 12.9, 9.5, 7.3, 12.3, 11.5 and 11.8%, and the report's text confirms 11.8% for 2025. The people who left are chart labels read the same way.

**Checks in the package** (`extract.py` exits non-zero and writes nothing if one fails): the three contribution parts add up to the printed total in every statement column; increases less decreases equal the printed net change, and the figures behind each total add up to it; opening net assets plus the net change equal closing net assets, which equal the next year's opening; the members' balance rolls forward in the note; each served money value is compared with the same year's value in that year's own statement (statements as text exist for 2019, 2020, 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025; 2020 and 2025 are the served ones) and the 2025 report's counts with the earlier reports' (a difference is recorded in the report as a restatement or revision, not rejected; `test_extract.py` pins the recorded lists, so a new one fails the tests); the 2025 return equals the report text; the people-paid table's rows add up to its total row; and the ten anchors, the 99 rows and the manifest against the files. The report also records the CSV's SHA-256, which the Social import checks, so a value edited by hand in the CSV is rejected.

**Checks in `validateSocialFacts`:** a changed value, a missing, extra or duplicate observation, a year outside 2019–2025 and a zero where a statement prints no line are rejected; the three contribution parts must add up to All contributions every year; and the anchors must match (end of 2025 in members' accounts 8,237,530,000 GEL and end of 2019 508,031,000 GEL; total fund assets end of 2025 8,365,433,000 GEL; 2025 contributions 1,431,780,000 GEL of which the state budget 370,989,000 GEL; 2022 gains or losses on securities −31,187,000 GEL; members 1,705,365 at the end of 2025 and 958,226 at the end of 2019; employers 145,780; 2025 return 11.8%). The 99 values are 13 measures × 7 years and 2 measures (securities, dividends) × 4 years.

**Recorded restatements: none.** Every served money value was compared with the statement of its own year where one exists as text (2019, 2022, 2023, 2024); they are identical.

**Recorded revisions of counts and returns.** The Fund revises older figures. Each earlier annual report's figure for its own year was compared with the 2025 report's; the page serves the 2025 report's figure.

## Package, files and ids

The research package `docs/Raw Data/Social/ssa-statistics/` (SSA) and `docs/Raw Data/Social/geostat-social-protection/` (Geostat cross-check) holds the original workbooks and page captures with their fingerprints, `prepare.py` and `verify_independent.mjs`, the reviewed `area-mapping.csv` and `source-findings.csv`. It is immutable evidence; `npm run data:prepare-social` reads it, never the live SSA site. The on-disk size of the 1,027 SSA workbooks is 206 MB.

- `data/imports/social-protection-annual.csv`: the canonical file, one row per observation, with `year`, `indicator_id`, `geography_id`, `value`, `unit`, `months` (1 for a December count, 12 for a full year, fewer for a part year), `basis` (always `actual`), `value_status` (`official` or `calculated`), the source id, workbook, sheet and cell, and the review date. UTF-8 with BOM.
- `data/imports/social-protection-series-breaks.csv`: the two counting changes in the demography break format, read from the CSV and not mirrored.
- `data/reports/social-protection-validation.json`: the reconciliation evidence written with the file.
- `data/methodology/source-archives/social-protection.csv`: the public archive of the SSA topic pages and Geostat divisor files.

Ids are stable lowercase ASCII and label-independent. Indicators are `social.<programme>.<measure>`: `social.pension.recipients`, `.amount`, `.avg_monthly`; the same for `pension_idp` (no average) and `pension_highmountain`; `social.subsistence.families`, `.persons`, `.registered_families`, `.registered_persons`, `.employment_families`, `.amount`, `.employment_amount`, `.avg_monthly`, and `social.subsistence.persons_age_<band>` for SSA's eight age bands; `social.social_package.<group>.recipients` and `.amount` for the ten groups (`total`, `disability`, the four `disability_*` severities, `survivors`, `war_participants`, `political_repression`, `other`) and `social.social_package.<group>.avg_monthly` for the same ten groups; and `social.payments.total.amount` plus `social.payments.<programme>.amount` for SSA's 18 programmes. Geography ids are `country.georgia`, `region.*`, the two-digit municipality code (Tbilisi only as `region.tbilisi`) and `unit.akhalgori` and `unit.upper_abkhazia`. Calculated page figures are never in the file: a share of residents has the id `<indicator>#share` and a price-adjusted average `<indicator>#real`, only in the page's state and Excel.

## Validation and serving

`npm run data:prepare-social` writes the canonical file (UTF-8 with BOM), the break register and `data/reports/social-protection-validation.json`; `--check` reproduces them and runs in `data:validate`. The research package's `prepare.py --check`, `verify_independent.mjs` (every served cell re-read with SheetJS) and unit tests must have passed. Validation rejects a changed value, a missing, extra or duplicate observation, a year outside 2013–2025 (2019–2025 for the funded pension rows), a 12-month sum with fewer months than SSA published and a zero where SSA's cell is blank, and checks that money equals its monthly cells, places add up to their region and Georgia, the age bands add up to the people in receiving families in every place, groups add up to the total (in December counts, money and the monthly-count sums behind the group averages), every average equals its money ÷ its monthly-count sum, the withheld averages are exactly those whose counts cover other months than their money (the reviewed register `SOCIAL_AVERAGE_GAPS`, held equal to the data both ways), All programmes equals its rows, and the anchors match (2025: 888,518 pensioners, 4,262,309,090 GEL of pensions, average pension 405.13 GEL; 133.23 GEL in 2013; 129,506 receiving families, 463,122 people, 54,842 employment-programme families, 397,144 registered families, 855,291,818 GEL of subsistence allowance, 187,764 social package recipients, 544,241,359 GEL of social package, 6,166,123,811 GEL across all programmes).

The Prisma mirror `SocialFact` (keyed by indicator, geography and year, with the source document it cites) is created by migration `20261020190000_social` (RLS on, public grants revoked) and filled only by the parity-checked `npm run data:import`, which compares every field inside the import transaction and logs `[OK ] SocialFact: csv=22087 db=22087`. The 25 `source.ssa_*` rows and the six `source.pension_fund_*` rows of `data/sources/source-documents.csv` arrive in the same import. CSV mode and database mode must serve identical values. The live migration and import happen only through the release pipeline (`docs/deployment.md`). Pages are prerendered. Like Health and Education, social protection has no `/mcp` tool or central JSON/CSV publication, and its sources stay out of the fact-query snapshot. Excel downloads follow the selected page, place, heading, series and years, and hold Summary, Data and Sources sheets in the reader's language, with blank missing cells, the "calculated by Fiscal.ge" note when a calculated value is included, and validated links to the archived SSA topic pages and Geostat files on fiscal.ge.
